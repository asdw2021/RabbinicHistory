ר׳ מנחם מענדל שניאורסון

Menachem Mendel Schneerson
1902–1994
Known as: הרבי מליובאוויטש
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ מנחם מענדל שניאורסון (18 April 1902–12 June 1994), the seventh Lubavitcher Rebbe, transformed Chabad-Lubavitch from a displaced Hasidic court into an international network of religious education, outreach and social institutions. Born in Nikolaev, he grew up in Yekaterinoslav, where his father ר׳ לוי יצחק שניאורסון served as rabbi and kabbalist under the Russian Empire and Soviet rule. He married Chaya Mushka, daughter of ר׳ יוסף יצחק שניאורסון, in Warsaw in 1928. The couple lived in Berlin and Paris; he studied university subjects in Berlin and earned an engineering diploma in Paris in 1937, while continuing Torah study and work for his father-in-law. They escaped occupied France and reached New York in 1941. He directed Merkos L’Inyonei Chinuch, Machne Israel and Kehot before accepting Chabad leadership in 1951. From 770 Eastern Parkway he expanded shlichut, sending couples to build enduring Jewish institutions, and promoted public mitzvah campaigns, Torah publication, women’s education and use of modern communications for religious purposes. His talks and letters address halakhah, Hasidic thought, biblical interpretation, science, education and public affairs. In his final years, messianic expectation intensified among followers; after his death without a successor, interpretations of those expectations remained contested within Chabad. His institutional system nevertheless continued expanding globally.
