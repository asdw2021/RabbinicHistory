ר׳ יחיא יצחק הלוי

Yihya Yitzhak Halevi
1867–1932
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ יחיא יצחק הלוי (c. 1867–1932) was chief jurist, chief rabbi and communal leader of Yemenite Jewry during a period of famine, political change and internal controversy. Born in Sana’a to Musa Yitzhak Halevi, a tanner, he studied first with his maternal grandfather ר׳ שלום מנצורה and later in the Maharitz synagogue and the beit midrash of ר׳ חיים קורח. Trained in Talmud, halakhah, ritual slaughter and communal administration, he became head of the Sana’a rabbinical court in 1902 and hakham bashi by 1905. After the devastating siege and famine of 1904–06, he rebuilt communal structures, supervised judges and slaughterers across Yemen and represented Jewish communities before Imam Yahya, including collection of the jizya. He corresponded with leaders abroad, among them ר׳ אברהם יצחק הכהן קוק, and dealt with emigration, education and the rescue of Jewish orphans threatened with forced conversion. Although he opposed the Dor De’ah reforms associated with ר׳ יחיא קאפח and defended established Kabbalistic custom, the two continued serving together on the court. Thousands of his rulings, letters and legal documents survive in manuscript and were later gathered in the איש ימיני memorial series. He died in Sana’a in 1932.
