ר׳ ירוחם הלוי ליבוביץ

Yerucham HaLevi Leibovitz
1875–1936
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ ירוחם הלוי ליבוביץ (c.1875–1936), known as the Mashgiach of Mir, was one of the formative Musar educators of the twentieth-century yeshiva world. Born in Lyuban in present-day Belarus, he studied at Slabodka under ר׳ נתן צבי פינקל and then at Kelm, whose discipline and intellectual Musar became central to his teaching. After marrying Rivka, a relative of ר׳ שמחה זיסל זיו, he spent extended periods studying at Kelm and in the Kodashim kollel at Radin, where he became mashgiach in 1907. Concerned that his prominence might impinge upon ר׳ ישראל מאיר הכהן, he left Radin and joined Mir around 1910–1911. During the First World War he was separated from the yeshiva’s Poltava exile and subsequently helped rebuild several Lithuanian institutions, including Slabodka and Ponevezh. He returned permanently to Mir in 1923–1924, inaugurating what later students remembered as the yeshiva’s spiritual golden age. His exacting talks united Kelm’s analysis of human motives with a sweeping view of human dignity, divine order, and responsibility. Students included ר׳ יחזקאל לוינשטיין, ר׳ חיים שמואלביץ and many later postwar leaders. He died in Mir on 18 Sivan 5696. His family and students edited his talks as דעת תורה, דעת חכמה ומוסר and related collections.
