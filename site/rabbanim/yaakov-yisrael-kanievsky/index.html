ר׳ יעקב ישראל קניבסקי

Yaakov Yisrael Kanievsky
1899–1985
Known as: הסטייפלר

ר׳ יעקב ישראל קניבסקי (17 June 1899–10 August 1985), commonly called the Steipler and בעל קהלות יעקב, was a Novardok-trained Talmudist, yeshiva teacher, author, and influential leader of Lithuanian Haredi Judaism in Israel. He was born in Tashan, Ukraine, to ר׳ חיים פרץ קניבסקי and Bracha; after his father’s death the family returned to Hornostaypil, the town from which his enduring sobriquet was derived. He entered the Novardok yeshiva network as a boy, followed it east to Homel during the First World War, endured conscription into the Red Army, and later crossed into Poland to join its Białystok centre. In 1927 he married Pesha Miriam Karelitz, sister of ר׳ אברהם ישעיהו קרליץ, and served as a senior lecturer at Beit Yosef–Novardok in Pinsk. In 1934 he immigrated to Mandatory Palestine and became head of the movement’s Beit Yosef yeshiva in Bnei Brak. His analytical studies, first issued in separate volumes and later reorganized as קהלות יעקב on the Talmud, became his principal scholarly legacy. He also wrote on biblical interpretation, faith, ethics, Torah study, and halakhic measurements; his correspondence was collected posthumously. Although increasingly consulted for counsel and communal guidance, he devoted most of his time to study, writing, teaching, and receiving petitioners. He died in Bnei Brak and was buried beside ר׳ אברהם ישעיהו קרליץ in the Shomrei Shabbos cemetery.
