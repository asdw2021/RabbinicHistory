ר׳ דונש בן לברט

Dunash Ibn Labrat
920–990
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ דונש הלוי בן לברט, active in the mid-to-late tenth century, was a Hebrew poet, grammarian, biblical philologist and polemicist whose adaptation of Arabic quantitative meter transformed Hebrew poetry in al-Andalus. ר׳ משה אבן עזרא identifies him as born in Fez, although modern reference scholarship allows Baghdad as another possibility and places his birth around 920–925. His family had Iraqi connections, and as a young man he studied in Baghdad with ר׳ סעדיה גאון. He later recalled presenting metrically innovative Hebrew verse to his teacher. By the middle of the century he entered the Córdoba circle patronized by ר׳ חסדאי אבן שפרוט. There he challenged the מחברת lexicon of ר׳ מנחם בן סרוק in a formally composed series of objections, criticizing linguistic analyses that he believed could distort Scripture, halakhah and belief. Students of ר׳ מנחם replied, and ר׳ יהודי בן ששת defended ר׳ דונש, creating one of the foundational controversies of medieval Hebrew philology. His poetic innovation applied Arabic distinctions between long and short syllables to biblical Hebrew. Opponents saw the system as foreign to the sacred language, yet it became a basis of the classical Andalusian Hebrew school. His surviving corpus includes secular and liturgical poems, riddles, grammatical polemics and criticisms of positions associated with his own teacher ר׳ סעדיה גאון. The Sabbath hymn דרור יקרא and wedding poem דוי הסר are traditionally and widely attributed to him. A poignant poem by his unnamed wife, preserved in the Cairo Genizah, suggests a painful separation when he departed, but it does not provide a secure itinerary. Later details of his life are sparse: evidence places him alive after 985, while the conventional death date around 990 and Córdoba as the place of death remain unverified.
