ר׳ יהודה בן יוסף אבן אלפכאר

Yehudah AlFakhar
1170–1235
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ יהודה בן יוסף אבן אלפכאר (born in the last third of the twelfth century; died 1235) was a Toledo physician, communal notable and learned opponent of the Maimonidean philosophical project. He belonged to the distinguished Alfakhar family, whose Arabic name al-Fakhkhār means ‘the potter’. The family originated in Granada and was established in Toledo by the twelfth century, probably amid the dislocations produced by Almohad rule. His father, ר׳ יוסף אלפכאר, was a physician at the court of Alfonso VIII and an influential Jewish leader; an older relative, ר׳ אברהם אלפכאר, likewise served the Castilian court. ר׳ יהודה inherited this unusually powerful combination of scientific learning, court access and communal standing. Medieval and modern accounts describe him as an accomplished physician and identify him, though with some caution, as physician-in-ordinary to Ferdinand III of Castile. His surviving importance, however, rests chiefly on the Maimonidean controversy of 1232–1235. When supporters of ר׳ משה בן מימון sought allies against the anti-philosophical campaign arising in Montpellier, the elderly grammarian and exegete ר׳ דוד קמחי travelled from Provence toward Toledo in hopes of winning ר׳ יהודה’s support. Illness interrupted the journey, and ר׳ דוד instead opened a correspondence. The exchange revealed that ר׳ יהודה was not an uneducated enemy of secular knowledge: he possessed medical and philosophical learning, admired ר׳ משה בן מימון’s greatness and halakhic achievement, yet rejected the authority granted to speculative Aristotelian premises when they appeared to override the plain force of Scripture and rabbinic tradition. He particularly attacked allegorical interpretation and denied that reverence for a great scholar required acceptance of every conclusion. His replies, written in learned, densely allusive rhymed Hebrew prose, also contained sharp personal attacks on ר׳ דוד קמחי; their severity drew criticism even among sympathizers. The correspondence survives in later manuscripts and was printed in אגרות קנאות. It is a crucial primary witness to the fact that the 1230s controversy was not simply a contest between reason and ignorance, but a dispute among highly educated Jewish scholars over the limits of philosophical interpretation, educational authority and fidelity to inherited texts. No independent medical treatise or philosophical summa by ר׳ יהודה is securely known. He probably died in Toledo in 1235. Because the documentation anchors his career in Toledo and supplies no reliable personal journey elsewhere, this record does not convert the travels of ר׳ דוד קמחי or the wider circulation of the controversy into movements by ר׳ יהודה himself.
