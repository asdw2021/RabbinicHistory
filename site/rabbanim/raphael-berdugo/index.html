ר׳ רפאל בירדוגו

Raphael Berdugo
1747–1821
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ רפאל בירדוגו (1747–1821), revered in Moroccan tradition as המלאך רפאל, was a dayan, halakhist, biblical commentator and communal leader of Meknes. He was born into the Berdugo scholarly dynasty, the son of ר׳ מרדכי בירדוגו and a descendant of ר׳ משה בירדוגו, the משביר. Orphaned while young and living for years in severe poverty, he developed an unusually broad literary programme: responsa and legal decisions, commentaries on Scripture and the Talmud, studies of the Shulchan Aruch, sermons, grammar and a Judeo-Arabic Torah explanation intended to make the plain sense accessible. By the late eighteenth century he sat on the Meknes rabbinical court and became its dominant authority. His public activity extended beyond adjudication to communal regulations on inheritance, family expenditure and governance during the famine, epidemic and political violence that afflicted Moroccan Jewry under Sultan Yazid. He sometimes challenged local notables and fellow judges, showing that the honorific attached to him did not imply an uncontroversial career. His responsa preserve valuable evidence about everyday economic and family life in precolonial Morocco. He died in Meknes on Hoshana Rabbah 5582. His legal and exegetical writings remained central to later Moroccan rabbinic scholarship, many reaching print only after his death.
