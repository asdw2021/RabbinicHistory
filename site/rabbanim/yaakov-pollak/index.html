ר׳ יעקב בן יוסף פולק

Yaakov Pollak
1460–1541
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ יעקב בן יוסף פולק (c.1460–1541?) was the first great rosh yeshiva of early modern Polish Jewry and the figure most associated with the analytical method called חילוקים, later labelled pilpul. His birthplace is uncertain. He studied with ר׳ יעקב מרגליות of Nuremberg and around 1490 served as rabbi and rosh yeshiva in Prague alongside ר׳ יצחק מרגליות. A bitter 1492 dispute over a minor’s annulment of marriage brought opposition from ר׳ יהודה מינץ and others and eventually pushed him from Prague. In Kraków he established what YIVO describes as Poland’s first major Talmudic academy, transferring the German-Bohemian hillukim method into Polish learning. King Alexander appointed him in 1503 as rabbi of Poland or Lesser Poland, an unusually senior communal office. Internal disputes later led him to leave Kraków. Evidence for his final decades is fragmentary: a 1532 approbation may place him in Constantinople en route to Jerusalem, and traditions place him in the Land of Israel before a return to Poland. His outstanding student ר׳ שלום שכנא transmitted his method to the generation of ר׳ משה איסרליש. He deliberately left no systematic book, reportedly resisting the idea that later generations should be bound by written rulings. His conventional death date is 1541, though the attribution of a Lublin grave is doubtful.
