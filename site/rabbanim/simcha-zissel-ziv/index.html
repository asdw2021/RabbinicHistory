ר׳ שמחה זיסל זיו

Simcha Zissel Ziv
1824–1898
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ שמחה זיסל זיו (1824–1898), known as the Alter or Saba of Kelm, was among the principal architects of the Musar movement. Born in Kelmė as Simcha Mordechai Ziskind Broida, he travelled to Kovno to study with ר׳ ישראל סלנטר and became one of his foremost disciples. He absorbed Musar’s disciplined programme of ethical self-formation. Around 1865 he established the Kelm Talmud Torah, combining intensive Torah study, moral reflection, order, and exacting personal conduct. Government pressure led him to transfer the institution to Grobiņa in Courland in 1880; ill health brought him back to Kelm the following year, though the Grobiņa branch continued until 1886. His Kelm school trained an influential circle that carried Musar into Lithuanian yeshivot and, from 1892, into Ottoman Palestine. He avoided a formal rabbinic office and taught through personal example, carefully structured study, letters, and discourses. His teachings were assembled posthumously in חכמה ומוסר and כתבי הסבא מקלם. He died in Kelm on 26 July 1898, the eve of Tisha B’Av.
