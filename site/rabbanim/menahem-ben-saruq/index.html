ר׳ מנחם בן סרוק

Menahem Ben Saruq
920–970
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ מנחם בן יעקב אבן סרוק (conventionally c. 920–c. 970) was a Hebrew poet, court secretary, biblical lexicographer and one of the founders of scientific Hebrew philology in al-Andalus. Born at Tortosa, he went while young to Córdoba and entered the circle of ר׳ יצחק בן עזרא אבן שפרוט. After his patron's death he returned for a time to Tortosa, where he engaged in commerce, before ר׳ חסדאי אבן שפרוט summoned him back to Córdoba as secretary and literary collaborator. He composed court poetry and correspondence—including, according to the standard biographical tradition, the Hebrew letter sent in ר׳ חסדאי's name to Joseph, king of the Khazars—and produced the מחברת מנחם at his patron's encouragement. This pioneering Hebrew-language dictionary arranged the vocabulary of the Hebrew Bible, together with biblical Aramaic, by lexical roots. Its decision to explain Hebrew in Hebrew made it usable beyond Arabic-speaking lands, while its analysis allowed roots of varying length rather than consistently identifying the triliteral system later established by Andalusian grammarians. Around the middle of the tenth century ר׳ דונש בן לברט presented a large set of criticisms to ר׳ חסדאי, alleging linguistic errors and, polemically, interpretations resembling Karaite readings. The accusations should not be treated as proof that ר׳ מנחם was a Karaite. The controversy cost him his patron's protection: his complaint letter describes humiliation, violence against his household and confinement, and later biographical accounts report that he returned to Tortosa. His students defended the מחברת, and the debate became a formative episode in medieval Hebrew grammar. Although later grammarians displaced much of his root theory, his dictionary remained influential in Christian Europe and was repeatedly cited by ר׳ שלמה יצחקי (רש״י); ר׳ יעקב תם later wrote הכרעות in its defense. His final years and place of death are not securely documented.
