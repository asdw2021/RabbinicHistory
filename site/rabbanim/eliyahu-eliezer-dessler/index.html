ר׳ אליהו אליעזר דסלר

Eliyahu Eliezer Dessler
1892–1953
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ אליהו אליעזר דסלר (1892–1953) was a Belarus-born British and Israeli mussar teacher and religious thinker. Born in Homel to ר׳ ראובן דב דסלר, he was formed in the disciplined Kelm Talmud Torah, where the legacy of ר׳ שמחה זיסל זיו shaped his ethical psychology. After marriage and years of economic instability, he went to England in 1929. He taught in London’s East End and Dalston and helped create institutions for intensive Torah learning. In the early 1940s he became a founder and guiding spirit of the Gateshead Kollel, a decisive institution in the reconstruction of European Torah scholarship in Britain. In 1947 ר׳ יוסף שלמה כהנמן invited him to become mashgiach of the newly rebuilt Ponovezh Yeshiva in Bnei Brak. His talks combined Kelm mussar, kabbalistic themes and a distinctive account of free will, self-interest, giving and spiritual perception. He published little as a conventional author during his lifetime. After his death, students ר׳ אריה כרמל and ר׳ חיים פרידלנדר assembled letters, notes and talks as מכתב מאליהו, which became one of the most influential works of twentieth-century Jewish thought. He died in Bnei Brak on 31 December 1953 and was buried there.
