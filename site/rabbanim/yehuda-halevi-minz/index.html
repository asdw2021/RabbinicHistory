ר׳ יהודה הלוי מינץ

Yehuda HaLevi Minz
1405–1508
Known as: מהר״י מינץ
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ יהודה בן אליעזר הלוי מינץ (c.1405–1508), known as מהר״י מינץ, was the leading Ashkenazi rabbinic authority of Renaissance Italy. Born at Mainz, from which the family name derives, he received an Ashkenazi Talmudic education before moving to northern Italy around 1462. At Padua he founded or reorganized a yeshiva on the German model and served as rabbi for roughly forty-seven years. The academy attracted students from across Europe and helped make Padua a major centre of rabbinic learning. Among the scholars connected to his school were his son ר׳ אברהם מינץ and his descendant by marriage ר׳ מאיר קצנלבוגן, who later preserved his responsa. ר׳ יהודה issued communal ordinances, adjudicated marriage and divorce questions and defended established rabbinic authority in disputes such as his controversy with Elijah Delmedigo. A later claim that he lectured in philosophy at the University of Padua is disputed and may confuse him with Delmedigo, so it is not treated here as fact. Much of his writing was reportedly destroyed during warfare and upheaval. Sixteen responsa and several rulings survived and were printed with the responsa of ר׳ מאיר קצנלבוגן. He died at Padua on 27 September 1508, at an exceptionally advanced age according to contemporary tradition.
