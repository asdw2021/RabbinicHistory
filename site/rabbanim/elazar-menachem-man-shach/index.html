ר׳ אלעזר מנחם מן שך

Elazar Menachem Man Shach
1899–2001
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ אלעזר מנחם מן שך (1899–2001) was a Lithuanian-born Israeli Talmudist, rosh yeshiva and central leader of non-Hasidic Haredi Judaism. Born in Vabalninkas, he entered the Panevėžys yeshivah as a child and continued in Slabodka, Slutsk and Kletsk amid war, revolution and institutional displacement. His principal teachers included ר׳ איסר זלמן מלצר and ר׳ אהרן קוטלר. He immigrated to Mandatory Palestine in 1940, taught in Jerusalem, Petah Tikva and Rehovot, and joined the senior faculty of Ponovezh Yeshiva in Bnei Brak in the early 1950s. There his analytical lectures and personal authority shaped generations of Lithuanian yeshivah students. His אבי עזרי offers novellae on ר׳ משה בן מימון’s משנה תורה, while later collections preserve letters, public addresses and mussar thought. From the 1970s he became an influential communal and political leader, helping sponsor Shas in 1984 and founding Degel HaTorah in 1988 after conflict within Agudath Israel. His positions on Zionism, secular culture, military service and party politics were often forceful and controversial. He died in Tel Aviv in 2001 and was buried in Bnei Brak after an exceptionally large funeral.
