ר׳ עמרם בר ששנא גאון

Amram Gaon
810–875
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ עמרם בר ששנא גאון (fl. ninth century; conventionally c.810–c.875) was a leading Babylonian halakhist and head of the academy of Sura–Mata Mehasia. A pupil of ר׳ נטרונאי בן הילאי, he was already involved in academy leadership before his principal tenure. The epistle of ר׳ שרירא גאון preserves a complicated succession: ר׳ עמרם had earlier been called Gaon during an institutional dispute, stepped aside, and later led for eighteen years. His fame rests especially on סדר רב עמרם גאון, the earliest surviving comprehensive Jewish prayer order. It originated as a responsum sent from Mata Mehasia to ר׳ יצחק בר ר׳ שמעון and a Spanish community, combining the annual cycle of prayers with laws, explanations, and academy custom. Its opening identifies him as head of the academy and sends greetings from ר׳ צמח, av bet din, and the scholars and students. The surviving medieval manuscripts contain later additions, so modern scholars distinguish the original ninth-century response from accumulated liturgical layers. Genizah fragments and later collections also preserve his responsa on ritual, civil, and communal law, demonstrating the international reach of the Babylonian academies. His liturgical rulings profoundly influenced later siddurim and the prayer rites of Spain, Provence, France, and Ashkenaz.
