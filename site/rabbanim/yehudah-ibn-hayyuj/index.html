ר׳ יהודה בן דוד חיוג׳

Yehudah Ibn Hayyuj
945–1000
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ יהודה בן דוד חיוג׳ (Arabic: Abū Zakariyyā Yaḥyā ibn Dāwūd al-Fāsī; c. 945–c. 1000) was the decisive early theorist of scientific Hebrew grammar and one of the most influential biblical philologists of medieval Judaism. Born in Fez, he moved to Córdoba around 960, when the controversy between the schools of ר׳ מנחם בן סרוק and ר׳ דונש בן לברט was at its height. An older tradition identifies him with a Judah ben David who joined two other students in defending ר׳ מנחם, but modern Encyclopaedia Judaica regards that identification as doubtful; it should therefore not be treated as a securely documented teacher–student relationship. Whatever his precise factional role, his mature grammar departed from the variable one-, two- and three-letter root analyses associated with ר׳ מנחם and supplied a systematic explanation of Hebrew verbal morphology using concepts adapted from Arabic grammatical science. His central insight was that Hebrew verbal roots are fundamentally triliteral even when one radical is weak, quiescent, elided or assimilated. This allowed forms previously treated as irregular or derived from short roots to be analyzed according to stable principles. Modern linguistic scholarship cautions that his greatest innovation was not merely saying that roots have three consonants, an idea with precedents, but formulating how quiescent or weak radicals behave through conjugation, syllable structure, pronunciation and orthography. He wrote in Judaeo-Arabic. The two treatises that established his reputation analyze weak-letter verbs and geminate verbs; a third work treats vocalization, Masoretic and grammatical questions; and כתאב אלנתף applies linguistic analysis to difficult passages in the Former and Latter Prophets. That exegetical work appears to have remained unfinished and survives only in fragments. His works were rapidly transmitted across the Jewish world and translated into Hebrew more than once, notably by ר׳ משה הכהן אבן ג׳יקטילה and ר׳ אברהם אבן עזרא. ר׳ יונה אבן ג׳נאח expanded and corrected his system, building the comprehensive grammar and lexicon that became the mature expression of Andalusian philology. Later Hebrew grammarians and biblical commentators inherited both his analytical framework and much of the terminology produced through translation. Biographical detail beyond Fez and Córdoba is extremely sparse; no responsible itinerary can assign him additional travels or offices. His historical importance lies primarily in the chronological development of his works and their transformation of Hebrew linguistic analysis.
