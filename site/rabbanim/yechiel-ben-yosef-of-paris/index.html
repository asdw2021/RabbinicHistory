ר׳ יחיאל בן יוסף מפריז

Yechiel ben Yosef of Paris
1190–1268
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ יחיאל בן יוסף מפריז was a leading French Tosafist, head of the Paris academy, and defender of the Talmud. Born near the end of the twelfth century, probably in Meaux, he studied under ר׳ יהודה שירליאון and succeeded him around 1225. His Paris yeshiva reportedly attracted hundreds of students; among those associated with it was ר׳ מאיר מרוטנבורג. In 1240, after the apostate Nicholas Donin accused the Talmud before Louis IX’s court, ר׳ יחיאל בן יוסף מפריז served as the principal Jewish disputant. The proceedings culminated in the public burning of wagonloads of Hebrew manuscripts in Paris in 1242, a catastrophe for northern French learning. He continued teaching and communal fundraising under mounting pressure. Around 1260 he emigrated with family and disciples to Acre, where he founded an academy that transmitted the French Tosafist tradition in the Land of Israel. His rulings and Tosafot survive through citations and collections rather than one securely authored comprehensive book. He is commonly said to have died in Acre in 1268, although alternate chronologies and competing burial traditions require caution. No authenticated portrait exists.
