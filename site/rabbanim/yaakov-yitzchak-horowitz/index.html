ר׳ יעקב יצחק הלוי הורוביץ

Yaakov Yitzchak Horowitz
1745–1815
Known as: החוזה מלובלין
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ יעקב יצחק הלוי הורוביץ (c. 1745–1815), universally known as the חוזה מלובלין, was the most influential early Hasidic leader in central Poland. Born into a distinguished levitical rabbinic family, he studied first within traditional rabbinic culture and then entered Hasidism through ר׳ דוב בער ממזריטש, ר׳ שמלקה הורוביץ מניקלשבורג and, most decisively, ר׳ אלימלך מליז׳נסק. With his teacher’s blessing he began leading followers in the 1780s, initially at Łańcut and other Galician-Polish centres before establishing his court in Lublin. His reputation for spiritual perception produced the title ‘the Seer,’ but historical scholarship distinguishes his documented leadership from later miracle tales. His Lublin court helped turn Hasidism into a mass Polish movement and trained an extraordinary generation, including ר׳ יעקב יצחק רבינוביץ מפשיסחה, ר׳ שמחה בונים מפשיסחה, ר׳ דוד מלעלוב, ר׳ נפתלי צבי מרופשיץ, ר׳ צבי אלימלך מדינוב and ר׳ שלום רוקח מבעלז. Internal disagreements, especially with the Peshischa circle, reflected competing models of charismatic leadership and spiritual discipline. In 1814 he fell from a window under circumstances interpreted very differently by Hasidic memory and hostile satire; he remained ill and died on 9 Av 1815. His manuscripts and oral teachings were published posthumously in דברי אמת, זאת זכרון and זכרון זאת.
