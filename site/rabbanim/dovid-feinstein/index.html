ר׳ דוד פיינשטיין

Dovid Feinstein
1929–2020
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ דוד פיינשטיין (1929–6 November 2020) was an American rosh yeshivah, halakhic authority, communal leader and author. He was born in Lyuban, then in the Soviet Union and now in Belarus, to ר׳ משה פיינשטיין and Shima Kustanovich Feinstein. His father served as Lyuban's rabbi while maintaining religious life under increasingly hostile Soviet conditions. In January 1937 the family emigrated to New York and settled on Manhattan's Lower East Side, where ר׳ משה became head of Mesivtha Tifereth Jerusalem (MTJ). ר׳ דוד learned at MTJ under his father, later taught advanced classes there and became closely identified with both the yeshivah and the neighborhood in which he lived for the rest of his life. After his father's death in 1986, he succeeded him as rosh yeshivah of MTJ's elementary school, high school and kollel. He also became one of the most frequently consulted Orthodox decisors in the United States. The Orthodox Union sought his rulings on kashrut, youth work and new practical questions; rabbis and individuals approached him on ritual, commercial, medical and communal law. He served for decades on the Moetzes Gedolei HaTorah of Agudath Israel of America, belonged to Torah Umesorah's Vaad Roshei Yeshiva, and was involved with Chinuch Atzmai, Agudas Harabbonim and other institutions. His leadership was notable for reserve, accessibility and reluctance to cultivate public ceremony. His published Torah teaching appeared chiefly under the title קול דודי. English volumes adapted from his lectures cover the Torah, Haftarot, Esther and Ruth; other books address the Passover Seder and the structure and laws of the Jewish calendar. He also wrote the explanations of the Masoretic notes printed at the end of each weekly portion in ArtScroll's Stone Chumash. The publications often relied on compilers and adapters, so their bibliographic descriptions distinguish his authorship and lectures from editorial work by others. ר׳ דוד died in New York on 6 November 2020. After a funeral outside MTJ at 145 East Broadway, his coffin was flown to Israel; a second funeral left Jerusalem's Shamgar Funeral Home, and he was buried on Har HaMenuchot near ר׳ משה פיינשטיין. His significance lies in the combination of institutional continuity, practical halakhic judgment, wide communal responsibility and a deliberately understated personal manner.
