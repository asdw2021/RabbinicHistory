ר׳ משה בן נחמן (1194–c.1270), known as רמב״ן, Nachmanides, and in Catalan documents as Bonastruc ça Porta, was a Catalan Talmudist, halakhist, biblical commentator, kabbalist, communal leader, and physician. Born in Girona, he absorbed Spanish legal traditions together with the dialectical learning of the northern French Tosafists through teachers including ר׳ יהודה בן יקר and ר׳ נתן בן מאיר מטרינקטייל. His writings helped establish the dominant school of Talmudic interpretation in medieval Catalonia; major students included ר׳ שלמה בן אדרת and ר׳ אהרן הלוי. He sought a mediating position during the Maimonidean controversies while retaining substantive disagreements with ר׳ משה בן מימון. In 1263 King James I compelled him to represent Judaism against the convert Pablo Christiani at the Barcelona Disputation. His Hebrew account of the debate led to Dominican accusations and legal proceedings. After leaving the Crown of Aragon, he reached Acre and Jerusalem in 1267, helped renew organized Jewish worship in devastated Jerusalem, visited biblical sites, and later taught in Acre. His פירוש התורה integrates philology, midrash, halakhah, theology, geography, and guarded kabbalistic interpretation; he revised it after seeing the Land of Israel. His final years created a durable intellectual bridge between Catalonia and the eastern Mediterranean.
