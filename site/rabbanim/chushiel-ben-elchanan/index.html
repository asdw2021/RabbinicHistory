ר׳ חושיאל בן אלחנן

Chushiel Ben Elchanan
950–1027
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ חושיאל בן אלחנן (active late tenth–early eleventh century; conventionally c. 950–c. 1027) was a major Talmudist and head of the academy or bet midrash at Kairouan, whose teaching helped make the North African city an influential center of rabbinic scholarship. His origins are uncertain: Babylonia, Spain and Italy have all been proposed, but the strongest view identifies him with the scholar who says in an autograph Geniza letter that he came from a Christian country, most plausibly Italy. The same letter, written from Kairouan to ר׳ שמריה בן אלחנן in Fustat, describes his arrival from Italy and his being detained or persuaded to remain by the Kairouan community while awaiting his son Elhanan. This contemporary evidence conflicts with the much later narrative in ספר הקבלה of ר׳ אברהם אבן דאוד, according to which ר׳ חושיאל was one of four emissary-scholars captured at sea after sailing from Bari, sold in North Africa and ransomed at Kairouan. The captive story is historically important but cannot safely be used as a literal itinerary. In Kairouan, ר׳ חושיאל became head of the academy, probably after ר׳ יעקב בן נסים אבן שאהין. He brought learning associated with Italy and gave unusual weight to the Jerusalem Talmud and halakhic midrash alongside the Babylonian Talmud, strengthening Kairouan's scholarly independence while maintaining respected relations with the Babylonian Geonim. His known students included his son ר׳ חננאל בן חושיאל and ר׳ נסים בן יעקב; both later led the academy and transmitted his methods. No independent book by ר׳ חושיאל is securely known. His scholarship survives through explanations quoted by his students, particularly in ר׳ נסים's מפתח and in writings attributed to ר׳ חננאל, as well as through his autograph Geniza letter. Babylonian authorities praised him as a great sage and “mountain of Torah.” After his death, ר׳ שמואל הנגיד sent ר׳ חננאל a letter of consolation and an Aramaic elegy and ordered memorial observances in Granada, Lucena and Córdoba, demonstrating that his reputation extended across the western Mediterranean.
