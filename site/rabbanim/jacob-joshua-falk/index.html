ר׳ יעקב יהושע פאלק

Jacob Joshua Falk
1680–1756
Known as: פני יהושע
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ יעקב יהושע פאלק (1680–1756), known from his classic Talmud commentary as the פני יהושע, was a major Polish-German rabbi of the early Acharonic period. Born in Kraków, he studied locally and after marriage settled in Lviv. A catastrophic gunpowder explosion in 1702 killed his mother, first wife and young daughter; rescued from the ruins, he vowed to devote himself to a work elucidating Talmud and Rashi, the project that became פני יהושע. After serving Tarlow, Kurow and Lesko, he became chief rabbi of Lviv in 1717/18. Communal conflict led to further appointments in Buchach, Berlin, Metz and, from 1741, Frankfurt am Main. His analytical novellae became a standard yeshiva text. He also intervened forcefully in public controversies, opposing ר׳ יהונתן אייבשיץ during the amulet dispute; the conflict contributed to his departure from Frankfurt in 1750. He subsequently stayed in Worms and Offenbach while retaining strong ties to Frankfurt. Parts of פני יהושע appeared in Amsterdam in 1739 and Frankfurt in 1752 and 1756; the final part appeared posthumously in Fürth in 1780. He died in Offenbach on 16 January 1756 and was buried in Frankfurt’s Battonnstraße Jewish cemetery.
