ר׳ שמואל בן עלי הלוי

Shmuel Ben Ali
1120–present
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ שמואל בן עלי הלוי, known in Arabic contexts as Ibn al-Dastūr, was the dominant head of the Baghdad academy in the later twelfth century and one of the best-documented leaders of the post-classical Geonic succession. His origins and birth date are unknown. By 1164 he headed ישיבת גאון יעקב in Baghdad, issued responsa and appointments, and asserted jurisdiction across Iraq and into Iran and Syria. Surviving originals and copies of his Judaeo-Arabic letters, responsa and administrative acts occur in the Cairo Geniza and Firkovich collections. ר׳ בנימין מטודלה lists him as head of Baghdad's principal academy, while ר׳ פתחיה מרגנסבורג describes an elaborate court, large student body and broad authority; these travelers are indispensable but their numbers and ceremonial details should not be read uncritically. ר׳ פתחיה also reports that ר׳ שמואל's unnamed learned daughter taught Scripture to male students from within her home; his son-in-law ר׳ זכריה בן ברכאל later succeeded him. ר׳ שמואל's effort to strengthen academy authority brought conflict with the exilarchate, and his criticism of משנה תורה and of ר׳ משה בן מימון's formulation of resurrection produced a famous polemical exchange. Contrary to the conventional death date 1194, a document bearing his signature apparently survives from 1197. His career illuminates the institutional reach—and contested authority—of Baghdad's revived gaonate.
