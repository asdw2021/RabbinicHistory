ר׳ גרשום בן יהודה

Gershom ben Yehuda
960–1028
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ גרשום בן יהודה (c.960–c.1028), known as רבנו גרשום מאור הגולה, was the formative halakhist, teacher and communal authority of early Ashkenaz. His origins are uncertain—Metz is traditional—but his mature career was centered in Mainz, where his academy helped establish the Rhineland as an independent center of Talmudic learning as Babylonian institutional authority waned. His school produced Talmud commentaries, responsa, liturgical poetry and textual work, and trained the teachers of ר׳ שלמה יצחקי. Later Ashkenazic tradition associated him with communal enactments limiting polygamy, requiring a wife’s consent to divorce, protecting private correspondence and treating coerced converts with dignity. Modern scholarship cautions that the enactments accumulated and were transmitted under his authority rather than all arising at one datable synod. The exact contours of his writings and the date of his death remain debated, but his central role in the creation of Ashkenazic rabbinic culture is secure.
