ר׳ חסדאי אבן שפרוט

Chasdai Ibn Shaprut
915–970
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ חסדאי בן יצחק בן עזרא אבן שפרוט (c. 915–c. 970), often called חסדאי הנשיא, was a Jewish physician, diplomat, administrator, scholar and patron at the Umayyad court of Córdoba. His family came from Jaén and moved within the prosperous elite of Córdoba. Trained in medicine and languages—including Hebrew, Arabic and Romance or Latin—he entered the service of caliph Abd al-Rahman III as physician and gained increasing responsibility in customs administration, reception of embassies and foreign negotiation. He exercised ministerial influence but apparently did not formally bear the title of vizier. His diplomatic work included negotiations with León and with the embassy of Otto I, and his medical treatment of the deposed Sancho I became connected with an Umayyad-Navarrese plan that restored Sancho to León. When a Byzantine embassy brought a Greek manuscript of Dioscorides' pharmacological work to Córdoba, חסדאי participated in the multilingual team that rendered it into Arabic; older accounts that make him the sole translator overstate the evidence. He retained influence under al-Hakam II. For Jewish history, his court was transformative: he imported books, supported academies and scholars, and gathered figures including ר׳ מנחם בן סרוק and ר׳ דונש בן לברט, whose rivalry helped launch scientific Hebrew philology and the classical poetry of al-Andalus. He also supported ר׳ משה בן חנוך and the growth of independent Andalusian Torah scholarship. His Hebrew letter to Khazar king Joseph sought information about a sovereign Jewish kingdom and reflects both diplomatic networks and messianic-historical curiosity; the correspondence is genuine in broad scholarly consensus, though textual versions and transmission remain debated. חסדאי was not chiefly a rabbinic author, and this record therefore distinguishes his own letter and diplomatic activity from medical, literary and institutional projects he sponsored.
