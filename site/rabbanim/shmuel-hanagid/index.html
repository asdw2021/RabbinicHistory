ר׳ שמואל הלוי בן יוסף הנגיד

Shmuel HaNagid
993–1056
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ שמואל הלוי בן יוסף הנגיד (993–1055/56), known in Hebrew as ר׳ שמואל הנגיד and in Arabic as Abū Isḥāq Ismāʿīl ibn Naghrīla, uniquely combined rabbinic scholarship, Hebrew poetry, communal leadership, high government and military command in eleventh-century al-Andalus. Born in Córdoba to a prominent family originating in Mérida, he received broad Jewish and Arabic education and studied halakhah under ר׳ חנוך בן משה. Later accounts also associate his philological education with ר׳ יהודה בן דוד חיוג׳. When Berber forces captured and sacked Córdoba in 1013 during the civil war that destroyed the Umayyad caliphate, he fled to Málaga. The twelfth-century ספר הקבלה of ר׳ אברהם אבן דאוד tells how his elegant Arabic letters, written while he kept a spice shop, brought him to the attention of the Granadan vizier Abū al-Qāsim ibn al-ʿArīf. Whatever the story's literary shaping, he entered the Zirid administration, became secretary and adviser, and by 1027 was vizier to King Ḥabbūs ibn Māksan and acknowledged as nagid, or political and communal head, among the Jews. After Ḥabbūs died in 1038, ר׳ שמואל supported Bādīs in a contested succession and became the new king's chief minister and principal military official. For much of his remaining life he directed or accompanied Granada's armies in repeated campaigns against neighbouring Andalusian states. His dated battlefield poems are unusually important first-person evidence, though poetry must be read as crafted self-presentation rather than a neutral military chronicle. As nagid, he judged legal questions, led and supported Jewish scholarship, patronized poets and academies, copied and distributed books, and maintained networks extending to Kairouan and the eastern Geonic world. His relationship with ר׳ נסים בן יעקב included financial support and scholarly exchange; in 1049 his son ר׳ יהוסף הנגיד married ר׳ נסים's daughter. His הלכתא גברתא was an ambitious halakhic compilation drawing on both Talmuds, Midrash, Geonic rulings and independent analysis. His enormous Arabic biblical lexicon, כתאב אלאסתע׳נא, survives only in fragments and quotations. Above all, his Hebrew dīwān—organized around בן תהלים, בן משלי and בן קהלת—adapted Arabic meters and genres to biblical Hebrew and encompassed prayer, friendship, wine, love, wisdom, grief, satire, self-examination and war. His young son ר׳ יהוסף began organizing the poems under his supervision, leaving a rare authorially connected medieval collection. ר׳ שמואל died in 1055 or 1056 after years of campaigning; ר׳ יהוסף succeeded him, but his murder in the Granada violence of 1066 exposed how exceptional and precarious his father's political position had been.
