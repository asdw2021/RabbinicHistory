ר׳ אהרן קוטלר

Aharon Kotler
1892–1962
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ אהרן קוטלר (1892–1962), born Aharon Pines in Svislach, was a Lithuanian-trained rosh yeshiva who became a principal architect of postwar American yeshiva life. Orphaned young, he studied in Krynki and Minsk before entering Yeshivat Knesset Yisrael in Slabodka, where ר׳ נתן צבי פינקל and ר׳ משה מרדכי אפשטיין shaped his learning. After marrying Chana Perel, daughter of ר׳ איסר זלמן מלצר, he taught in Slutsk and from 1917 served as a regular maggid shiur. Communist pressure led him and many students across the border to Kletsk in 1921; there he built a major Polish-Lithuanian yeshiva and helped organize Vaad Hayeshivot. After Germany invaded Poland, the yeshiva regrouped in Vilna and then Jonava. Soviet authorities closed it in early 1941, and ר׳ אהרן escaped through Siberia and Japan to the United States. He immediately joined Vaad Hatzalah's efforts to rescue rabbis and yeshiva students trapped in Europe. In 1943 he founded Beth Medrash Govoha in Lakewood, deliberately locating intensive full-time Torah study away from New York's distractions. From a small opening group it became the institutional model for a large American kollel and yeshiva culture. He also led Agudath Israel of America's Council of Torah Sages, supported Torah Umesorah and helped establish and sustain Chinuch Atzmai in Israel. His surviving teachings—largely edited after his death as משנת רבי אהרן—combine close Talmudic analysis, lectures on Jewish thought, responsa and institutional correspondence. He died at Columbia-Presbyterian Medical Center in New York on 29 November 1962 and was buried on Har HaMenuchot in Jerusalem.
