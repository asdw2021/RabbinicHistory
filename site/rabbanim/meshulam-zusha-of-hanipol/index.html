ר׳ משולם זושא מאניפולי

Meshulam Zusha of Hanipol
1718–1800
Known as: ר׳ זושא מאניפולי
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ משולם זושא מאניפולי (c.1718–1800), widely known as ר׳ זושא, was an early Hasidic master celebrated for humility, ecstatic prayer, and radical trust in God. His precise birthplace and early chronology are uncertain; traditions connect him with Galicia, often Tarnów. He and his brother ר׳ אלימלך מליז׳נסק are remembered as wandering preachers before they entered the circle of ר׳ דוב בער המגיד ממזריטש. After the Maggid’s death in 1772, ר׳ זושא settled in Hanipol, where the Maggid was buried, and became an influential teacher in his own right. His reputation rests less on institutional leadership or formal authorship than on short teachings, stories, and a model of devotional self-effacement. He joined ר׳ יהודה לייב הכהן of Hanipol in endorsing the first publication of Tanya by ר׳ שניאור זלמן מליאדי. No book by him appeared during his lifetime; teachings attributed to him were collected posthumously in מנורת זהב and related compilations. Later portraits are imaginative, so the record deliberately carries no authenticated likeness.
