ר׳ בנימין בן יונה מטודלה

Binyamin of Tudela
1130–present
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ בנימין בן יונה מטודלה, conventionally known as Benjamin of Tudela, was a twelfth-century Jewish traveler and Hebrew travel writer from Tudela in Navarre. Almost everything known about him comes from ספר המסעות and its anonymous prologue. Although tradition calls him ר׳ בנימין and the prologue praises his learning in Torah and halakhah, no specific rabbinic office is documented. His occupation and purpose are likewise uncertain: scholars have proposed commerce, pilgrimage, practical guidance for Jewish travelers, investigation of diaspora communities and messianic interest, but none is conclusive. Probably departing around 1165–1166, he crossed Iberia, Provence, Italy, the Byzantine world, the eastern Mediterranean, the Crusader Levant, Syria and Mesopotamia, reaching Baghdad; Egypt and Sicily formed the best-attested return route before he reached Castile in 1173. He recorded distances, political conditions, monuments, trades, pilgrimage sites and especially Jewish populations, leaders and academies. His work carefully distinguishes some things seen from information heard from reputable informants, yet its population figures and its increasingly fabulous eastern material require caution. Modern scholarship generally treats the route through Baghdad—and probably parts of Persia, Egypt and Sicily—as substantially firsthand, while India, China, Central Asia and several distant communities derive largely or wholly from reports. The book remains an exceptional Hebrew witness to Jewish life and interregional connectivity before 1200.
