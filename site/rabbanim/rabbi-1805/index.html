ר׳ צבי הירש חיות (1805–1855), known as מהר״ץ חיות, was a Galician rabbi, Talmudist, halakhist, and historical scholar who sought to combine fidelity to rabbinic authority with disciplined investigation of the development of the Oral Torah. Born into a wealthy scholarly family in Brody, he studied under Galician authorities including ר׳ אפרים זלמן מרגליות and acquired broad linguistic and secular learning. He maintained important intellectual relationships with ר׳ נחמן קרוכמל and ר׳ שלמה יהודה רפפורט, though their views and personal relations did not always coincide. Appointed district rabbi of Żółkiew in 1829, he served there for more than twenty years. In response to Austrian regulations, he passed external examinations at the University of Lwów in 1846 and received official authorization to continue in the rabbinate—not, as sometimes claimed, a doctorate. His studies addressed rabbinic hermeneutics, the formation of the Talmuds and Midrashim, legal authority, communal enactments, textual criticism, and the boundary between tradition and historical scholarship. He opposed the Reform rabbinical conferences while also criticizing intellectual isolation within Orthodoxy. Elected rabbi of Kalisz in 1852, he encountered local opposition and remained three years. Illness brought him back to Galicia in 1855, and he died in Lwów. His הגהות מהר״ץ חיות became a standard feature of the Vilna Talmud.
