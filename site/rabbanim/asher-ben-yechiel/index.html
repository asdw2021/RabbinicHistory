ר׳ אשר בן יחיאל

Asher ben Yechiel
1250–1327
Known as: הרא״ש
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ אשר בן יחיאל (c.1250–1327), the Rosh, was a decisive bridge between the Tosafist tradition of Ashkenaz and the rabbinic culture of Christian Spain. Probably born in the Cologne region, he learned first from his scholarly family and became a leading disciple and dayan of ר׳ מאיר בן ברוך מרוטנבורג at Worms. After his teacher’s imprisonment in 1286, he helped lead German Jewry and dealt with the communal dislocation caused by the Rintfleisch massacres. Fearing arrest and confiscation, he left Germany in 1303 with his family, travelled through Provence, and stayed in Barcelona with ר׳ שלמה בן אברהם אדרת. On the latter’s recommendation he became rabbi and head of the Toledo academy in 1304–1305. There he transmitted Franco-German Talmudic analysis and communal custom while engaging Spanish legal realities and resisting what he regarded as excessive philosophical rationalism. פסקי הרא״ש, arranged along the Talmud and the digest of ר׳ יצחק אלפסי, became a central pillar of later halakhah. His responsa, Tosafot, and ethical צוואה likewise circulated widely. His son ר׳ יעקב בן אשר made his rulings foundational to ארבעה טורים, and they later entered שולחן ערוך. He died in Toledo in 1327.
