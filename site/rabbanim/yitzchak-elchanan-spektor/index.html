ר׳ יצחק אלחנן ספקטור

Yitzchak Elchanan Spektor
1817–1896
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ יצחק אלחנן ספקטור (1817–1896) was the pre-eminent halakhic authority and a central public leader of late imperial Russian Jewry. Born in Ros in the Grodno region, he studied first with his father and later under ר׳ בנימין דיסקין in Vawkavysk. After early rabbinates in Izabelin and Biaroza, he served successively in Nyasvizh and Novogrudok. In 1864 he became rabbi of Kovno, where his responsa, communal judgment and extensive correspondence gave him influence far beyond Lithuania. He worked with both traditional and secular communal leaders to oppose harmful state policies, secure kosher provisions for Jewish soldiers, defend Jewish education and organize famine and pogrom relief. He supported Hovevei Zion while maintaining a pragmatic approach to wider communal cooperation. His responsa Be'er Yitzchak and Ein Yitzchak and his Choshen Mishpat commentary Nachal Yitzchak became authoritative works. The Rabbi Isaac Elchanan Theological Seminary in New York, later central to Yeshiva University, was named in his memory.
