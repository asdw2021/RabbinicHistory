ר׳ יצחק בן ראובן אלברגלוני

Yitzhak AlBargeloni
1043–1113
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ יצחק בן ראובן אלברגלוני—also called ר׳ יצחק הברצלוני and, in later rabbinic literature, simply הרב אלברגלוני—was an early Catalan-Andalusian Talmudist, halakhic translator, dayyan and liturgical poet. He was born in Barcelona in 1043, when the city's Jewish scholarly culture was beginning to connect Christian Catalonia with the Arabic-speaking centres of al-Andalus. A Genizah fragment describes him as a pupil of a scholar identified in the modern reference tradition as ר׳ חנוך בן משה and therefore associates his formation with Córdoba. That identification presents a chronological difficulty, however, because the conventional death date assigned to the famous Cordoban ר׳ חנוך predates ר׳ יצחק's birth. The fragment may preserve a different chronology, a looser chain of discipleship or a mistaken identification; Córdoba is consequently retained as a qualified rather than certain stage. He later settled permanently in Dénia, a Mediterranean port then within Muslim al-Andalus, where he apparently served as a communal judge. Older sources say that he became connected, probably by marriage, to the Ibn Laktush family, but the exact relationship is not independently secure. His career joined Arabic and Hebrew literary cultures. In 1078, at age thirty-five, he translated ר׳ האי גאון's Arabic commercial-law treatise כתאב אלשרא ואלביע into Hebrew as ספר המקח והממכר. The translation made a major Geonic handbook on acquisition, sale, agency, defects, fraud and related monetary law available to Jews unable to read Arabic and remained in circulation for centuries. The Posen Library also attributes to him a Hebrew translation of כתאב אלאימאן, a treatise on oaths by ר׳ דוד בן סעדיה; this less famous translation should not be confused with his securely dated rendering of ר׳ האי גאון. He composed Talmudic explanations, including material on Ketubot and Eruvin, but these commentaries have not survived as complete independent works. His best-known original composition is the אזהרות beginning איזה מקום בינה, a large Shavuot poem enumerating the commandments according to the scheme of הלכות גדולות. Its 145 stanzas characteristically culminate in biblical quotations skillfully integrated into the syntax and rhyme. ר׳ יהודה אלחריזי later praised this technique, and the poem entered the Shavuot liturgy of numerous North African communities, including rites used in Algeria, Tunisia and Morocco. Additional securely associated poems include עלה משה לראש הר סיני, יום זה הוריד and יחיד נורא עלילה; פחדתי מיוצרי and יום זה מכפר לשבים are also attributed to him, while several further attributions proposed by nineteenth-century scholars remain disputed. ר׳ אברהם אבן דאוד counted him among the outstanding contemporary authorities named יצחק alongside ר׳ יצחק אלפסי and other Spanish scholars. ר׳ יהודה בן ברזילי הברצלוני has traditionally been called his pupil, but modern reference works treat that relationship as probable rather than certain. Later testimony also makes ר׳ משה בן נחמן a descendant of his family, though the precise genealogy is not recoverable. His enduring importance lies in carrying Geonic halakhah across a linguistic boundary, helping connect the learning of Córdoba and Muslim eastern Iberia with emerging Catalan scholarship, and creating a commandment poem whose North African liturgical life continued long after his own Talmudic writings were lost.
