ר׳ ברוך בער ליבוביץ

Baruch Ber Leibowitz
1862–1939
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ ברוך בער ליבוביץ (c. 1862–1939), renowned as Reb Baruch Ber and בעל ברכת שמואל, was a central transmitter of the Brisk analytical method. Born near Slutsk, he studied at Volozhin Yeshiva and became deeply attached to ר׳ חיים הלוי סולובייצ׳יק, whose conceptual approach shaped his lifelong teaching. After marriage to the daughter of ר׳ אברהם יצחק צימרמן, he served in Halusk. In 1904 he succeeded ר׳ חיים רבינוביץ as head of Yeshivat Knesset Beit Yitzchak in Slabodka. The First World War drove the institution through Minsk and Kremenchug; after the Bolshevik revolution he re-established it in Vilna. In 1926 Polish pressure over competing yeshivot led him to move the academy to Kamianets, where it became a leading prewar Lithuanian yeshiva. He travelled to the United States in 1928 with his son-in-law ר׳ ראובן גרוזובסקי to raise funds. His lectures, edited as ברכת שמואל, develop precise conceptual distinctions while repeatedly crediting his teacher. When war began in 1939, the yeshiva fled east into independent Lithuania. He became gravely ill and died in November 1939, shortly after reaching Vilna, where he was buried. His students carried his method into major postwar yeshivot.
