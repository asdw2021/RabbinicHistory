ר׳ סעדיה גאון (רס״ג)

Saadiyah Gaon
882–942
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ סעדיה בן יוסף אלפיומי (882–942), known as ר׳ סעדיה גאון or רס״ג, was the outstanding Jewish polymath of the Geonic period: head of the Sura academy, biblical translator and exegete, halakhist, philosopher, theologian, grammarian, lexicographer, liturgist, poet, calendar scholar and polemicist. Born in Egypt's Fayyum region, he wrote early Hebrew linguistic and anti-sectarian works before leaving Egypt around 915. He studied or worked in the Land of Israel, including the Tiberian world of Hebrew language and Masoretic learning, and then traveled east. In Aleppo in 921 he learned of ר׳ אהרן בן מאיר's proposed calendar calculation, and from Baghdad he participated in the 921–922 dispute between Palestinian and Babylonian authorities. Older historiography cast him as the decisive victor; recent Genizah scholarship argues that his role, while literarily important, was more limited. His defense of Rabbanite Judaism against Karaite and other challenges and his international reputation led the exilarch ר׳ דוד בן זכאי to appoint the Egyptian-born outsider Gaon of Sura in 928, helping revive the academy. Around 930 the two clashed when רס״ג refused to validate a disputed legal decision. Each attempted to depose the other, and after the exilarch's party prevailed, רס״ג spent several exceptionally productive years outside office in Baghdad. Reconciliation in 937 restored him to leadership. His Arabic Bible translation and commentary made Scripture accessible throughout the Judaeo-Arabic world, while אמונות ודעות created the first systematic Jewish philosophical theology of the medieval period, integrating revelation, rabbinic tradition, reason and forms of kalam argument. His corpus also transformed Hebrew philology, prayer, law, poetry and anti-Karaite debate. Many works survive only in Genizah fragments, so titles, dates and scope sometimes remain subjects of reconstruction.
