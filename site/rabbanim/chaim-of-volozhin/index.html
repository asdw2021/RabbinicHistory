ר׳ חיים איצקוביץ מוולוז׳ין

Chaim of Volozhin

Known as: ר׳ חיים מוולוז׳ין

ר׳ חיים איצקוביץ מוולוז׳ין (24 May 1749–14 June 1821), usually called ר׳ חיים מוולוז׳ין or Reb Chaim Volozhiner, was the leading disciple of ר׳ אליהו בן שלמה זלמן, the Vilna Gaon, and the institutional architect of the Lithuanian yeshivah tradition. Born in Volozhin to community leader ר׳ יצחק בן חיים and Rivka, he studied with ר׳ רפאל זיסקינד הכהן and ר׳ אריה לייב גינצבורג, author of שאגת אריה, before joining the Vilna Gaon’s circle. He served as rabbi of Volozhin, briefly held the rabbinate of Vilkomir/Ukmergė, and returned to his native town. In 1802 he circulated a public appeal for support; around 1803 he founded Yeshivat Etz Chaim, the Volozhin Yeshiva. Unlike the older local study-house model, it recruited advanced students from many communities, provided material support and organized continuous Torah study. It became the prototype for the great Lithuanian yeshivot. His posthumously published נפש החיים presents a kabbalistically informed theology of divine presence, prayer, mitzvot and especially Torah study; it engages ideas associated with Hasidism while articulating a distinct mitnagdic program. His son ר׳ יצחק מוולוז׳ין succeeded him and published נפש החיים. His teachings and institution shaped students including ר׳ זונדל מסלנט and enduring scholarly lineages associated with Volozhin, Brisk and the Musar movement.
