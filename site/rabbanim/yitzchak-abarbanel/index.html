ר׳ יצחק בן יהודה אברבנאל

Yitzchak Abarbanel
1437–1508
Known as: אברבנאל

ר׳ יצחק בן יהודה אברבנאל (1437–1508), commonly called Don Isaac Abarbanel, was a Portuguese Jewish statesman, financier, theologian, and biblical commentator whose life followed the upheavals of Iberian Jewry. Born in Lisbon to a distinguished Sephardi family, he received extensive Torah and philosophical education and succeeded his father as a financier and royal servant under Afonso V. In 1471 he helped ransom Jewish captives taken at Arzila. Accused after João II attacked the Braganza faction, he fled Portugal in 1483 and entered Castilian royal finance. In 1492 he tried unsuccessfully to avert the expulsion and left Spain with the exiles rather than accept conversion. At Naples he again served a royal court and resumed his commentaries, but the French invasion drove him through Messina and Corfu. From 1496 to 1503 he lived at Monopoli, devoting himself to exegetical, philosophical, and messianic writing, before settling in Venice, where he assisted negotiations with Portugal. His commentaries treat each biblical book as a literary and historical whole, posing structured questions and drawing on political experience, Jewish tradition, philosophy, and Christian exegesis. His works on redemption gave voice to the post-1492 exile. He died in Venice and was buried at Padua; the original grave location was later lost.
