ר׳ יהושע דוד פוברסקי

Yehoshua Dovid Povarsky
1902–1999
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ יהושע דוד פוברסקי (1902–1999), generally called ר׳ דוד פוברסקי, was a Lithuanian-style Talmudist, mussar teacher and rosh yeshiva who shaped more than five decades of learning at the rebuilt Ponovezh Yeshiva in Bnei Brak. Born David Kustonowitz in Lyuban to ר׳ שלום, he studied as a boy in Slutsk with ר׳ איסר זלמן מלצר. During the First World War he crossed wartime borders using papers bearing the name Yehoshua Povarsky; he retained that surname and added Yehoshua to his own name. His formative education passed through the displaced Slabodka and Mir yeshivot, the Kelm Talmud Torah and the prewar Ponovezh world. ר׳ ירוחם ליבוביץ profoundly influenced his mussar outlook. After marrying Tzipporah, daughter of ר׳ דוד דב קרייזר, he studied in the Baranovich circle of ר׳ אלחנן וסרמן and also delivered shiurim. He escaped Europe with his family in 1941 and initially headed the Tomchei Temimim yeshiva in Tel Aviv. In 1944 ר׳ יוסף שלמה כהנמן recruited him to lead the newly re-established Ponovezh Yeshiva beside ר׳ שמואל רוזובסקי; ר׳ אלעזר מנחם מן שך later joined its senior leadership. His teaching combined exact textual analysis, conceptual clarity, the disciplined ethical inheritance of Kelm and close personal responsibility for students. He avoided political prominence and centered his public life on daily learning and transmission. His Talmudic novellae and lectures were published in the multi-volume ישועת דוד and שיעורי ר׳ דוד, while ישמרו דעת and משכיל לדוד preserve his mussar discourses.
