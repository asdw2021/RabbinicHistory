ר׳ אלעזר בירבי קליר

Eleazar HaKalir
570–640
Known as: ר׳ אלעזר הקליר
Follow this story on the map

ר׳ אלעזר בירבי קליר, commonly called ר׳ אלעזר הקליר, was among the most influential early Palestinian payyetanim. His biography is exceptionally obscure: neither exact dates nor a securely identified hometown survive, and older theories placed him anywhere from tannaitic times to the tenth century. Linguistic study and discoveries in the Cairo Geniza now support a setting in Byzantine Palestine, probably in the sixth or early seventh century. He composed elaborate qedushta cycles and other liturgical poems for Sabbaths, festivals and fasts, signing his name and sometimes the formula ‘אלעזר בירבי קליר’ in acrostics. His compressed diction, neologisms, intricate rhyme, biblical tesserae and dense midrashic allusion created the style later called ‘Kaliric.’ Hundreds of poems or fragments are known; many entered Italian, Romaniote, Ashkenazic and other rites, including celebrated kinot for Tisha Be-Av and piyyutim for the High Holy Days and the Four Special Sabbaths. ר׳ אברהם אבן עזרא later criticized the difficulty of his language, yet Kalir’s structures shaped Hebrew worship poetry for centuries. The map therefore records a Palestinian literary context rather than invented journeys or offices.
