ר׳ אברהם בר חייא הנשיא

Avraham Bar Hiyya HaNasi
1065–1136

ר׳ אברהם בר חייא הנשיא (c. 1065–c. 1136), called Abraham Judaeus and Savasorda in Latin sources, was a Jewish mathematician, astronomer, calendrical scholar, philosopher and scientific translator active principally in Barcelona. His birthplace and early education are unknown; his command of Arabic science suggests formation within the intellectual culture of the northeastern Iberian frontier. The Arabic-derived title behind “Savasorda,” probably ṣāḥib al-shurṭa, and the Hebrew הנשיא indicate public and communal office, though their precise duties are uncertain. He wrote the earliest substantial mathematical and astronomical books in Hebrew, creating technical vocabulary for readers who did not know Arabic. חבור המשיחה והתשבורת presented practical geometry and a complete solution of quadratic equations; its 1145 Latin translation, Liber embadorum, influenced European mathematics, including Fibonacci. ספר העבור was the first Hebrew monograph devoted entirely to the Jewish calendar. צורת הארץ and חשבון מהלכות הכוכבים transmitted Ptolemaic-Arabic astronomy, while יסודי התבונה attempted a scientific encyclopedia. הגיון הנפש combined Neoplatonic cosmology, ethics and Jewish religious teaching; מגילת המגלה interpreted history and redemption through astrological chronology. He also collaborated with Plato of Tivoli in translating Arabic scientific works into Latin. Claims that he lived in Provence reflect works addressed to Provençal Jews but remain debated. His significance lies in making Arabic science available in both Hebrew and Latin and in fashioning a durable Hebrew scientific lexicon.
